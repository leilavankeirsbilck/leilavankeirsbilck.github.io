About me

I am an economist and applied mathematician. I completed my PhD in economics at UCLouvain (CORE/LIDAM) in September 2026, supervised by Francesca Monti and Sophie Béreau, after training as an engineer in applied mathematics at the same university.

My research brings networks and graph theory into macroeconometrics: high-dimensional Bayesian VARs structured by input–output linkages, and graph-based measures of systemic risk in financial networks. Since 2022 I have also been responsible for the inflation forecasts in Perspectives économiques, the quarterly macroeconomic outlook for the Belgian economy published by UCLouvain's Economic Analysis Service.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market and will attend the European Job Market in December 2026.

Research

Working Papers

  • Van Keirsbilck, L. & Monti, F. (2026). The Transmission of Shocks Across Sectors and the Dynamics of Sectoral Prices. ECB Working Paper Series, No. 3266. Also CEPR Discussion Paper No. 20621 and LIDAM Working Paper. This paper explores the dynamics of U.S. sectoral producer prices in a large Bayesian vector autoregressive model, where information from the input–output matrix is used to structure the long-run relationships between these time series.
  • Van Keirsbilck, L., Béreau, S. & Saerens, M. Mapping and Sampling Impacts on Centrality Measures' Ability to Explain Financial Institutions' Losses in Crisis Periods. PDF. This paper tests whether changes in the network mapping specification and changes in the sampling affect the econometric relation between losses and the systemic vulnerability of institutions, as measured by centrality.

Work in Progress

  • Van Keirsbilck, L. Import Tariffs and Sectoral Price Dynamics in an Input–Output BVAR for the United States. This project studies the impact of tariff shocks on U.S. sectoral producer prices, using the input–output-informed long-run BVAR framework. Draft available on request.
